# The project and its purpose

[Back to README](../README.md) · [Next: discovery](discovery.md)

Build a personal web presence you can develop throughout the course and use to market yourself. Your audience might be an internship team, a prospective collaborator, a community organization, or a future client. You do not need a finished professional identity on day one. The first task is to discover a credible direction and begin showing your work.

## Three parts, one outcome

**AI is a collaborator in the development process.** Use it to explore, explain, implement, and review. Your responsibility is to supply context, judge its suggestions, verify changes, and understand what you submit. This app does not need an AI service, API key, or chatbot to meet that objective.

**Docker provides the working environment.** It runs the application with its declared runtime and dependencies. Learn what the container is doing rather than treating the startup command as magic. It supports the portfolio work; it does not publish your site to the internet.

**The portfolio is the public-facing outcome.** It communicates your interests and abilities through projects, explanations, and evidence. The themes and learning pages give you material to explore while deciding how your own presentation should work.

## What to do in this phase

1. Start the existing application and identify the main pages.
2. Explain the relationship between the repository, Docker image, running container, and browser.
3. Complete a discovery brief: audience, opportunity, promise, evidence, voice, visual direction, and desired action.
4. Make one small, intentional change, such as your About introduction or a project summary.
5. Ask a peer what they understand about you from the result. Record what you would change next.
6. Document how AI helped and how you verified its contribution.

The current sample projects are briefs. Do not present them as completed research or client work. An honest small class exercise can be useful evidence when you explain your role, decisions, and limitations.

## A useful first checkpoint

Bring a running starter, your completed brief, a before-and-after example of an edit, and a short explanation of your Docker setup. Include an AI collaboration note describing one accepted suggestion, one suggestion you questioned, and the check you performed. These are suggested learning artifacts; your instructor sets submission requirements and grading.

A classmate should be able to answer: Who is this for? What does this student offer? What evidence supports that claim? What should I do next?

## Scope now and later

Use the existing local TypeScript content and page files. No CMS, database, authentication, blog engine, QR generator, analytics service, or external AI integration is required now. The [future vision](project-vision.md) describes possibilities after discovery. It is not a feature checklist for this phase.
