# Discover your direction

[Back to README](../README.md) · [Copy the brief template](discovery-brief-template.md)

Start with people and evidence before choosing a visual style. Your portfolio should give visitors a coherent reason to pay attention and a useful next step.

## 1. Choose an audience and opportunity

Name one primary audience and the opportunity you want. “Everyone who might hire me” is difficult to design for. “A small web team looking for a summer intern” gives you a more useful starting point. Describe what that reader needs to know to consider you.

## 2. Make a credible promise

Complete this sentence: “I help or contribute to ___ by ___, and I can demonstrate that through ___.” Use skills and evidence you actually have. If you are still learning, explain your direction and show your learning process.

Gather one or two artifacts: a class exercise, code contribution, sketch, prototype, written explanation, or community project. Record your role and collaborators. Separate an observed result from a hope or hypothesis.

## 3. Connect three communication tools

| Tool | What it contributes | Your decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brand archetypes | A vocabulary for personality and voice | How should my writing and presentation feel? |
| Design styles | Approaches to typography, hierarchy, space, color, and imagery | Which visual choices support my audience and message? |
| Cialdini's principles | Research-informed ways to think about persuasion | How can visitors assess my credibility and choose a next step? |

These tools have different foundations. Archetypes are creative storytelling prompts, not personality diagnoses. Design movements have histories and contexts, not just palettes. Persuasion principles describe tendencies, not guaranteed outcomes.

Explore the app's `/learn/archetypes`, `/learn/design-styles`, and `/learn/persuasion` pages. Select one primary archetype, a visual direction, and two relevant persuasion principles. Explain the relationship instead of picking three unrelated labels.

For example, a student seeking a UX internship could use a **Sage** voice to explain decisions, a **Swiss** hierarchy to make research easy to scan, and **authority** through documented methods. An actual collaborator's attributed feedback could supply **social proof**, with permission. Without that feedback, omit the quote; do not invent evidence to complete the framework.

A contrasting direction might use a **Creator** voice, expressive **New Wave** project covers, and **reciprocity** through a useful tutorial. Keep the reading experience clear even when the cover is experimental.

## 4. Define conversion in plain language

A conversion is the action you want a visitor to take: read a case study, inspect your code, start a conversation, or consider you for an opportunity. Choose one primary action for this phase. Make its label specific and its destination useful.

Use honest evidence, relevant context, and voluntary next steps. Invented testimonials, exaggerated roles, and false urgency weaken the credibility you are trying to build. You do not need every persuasion principle on every page.

## 5. Map a short visitor journey

Write the path you want someone to follow:

```text
Entry point → who I am and what I offer → project evidence → next action
```

For now, use existing pages. Future entry points might include a QR code on a résumé or a LinkedIn profile; you do not need to implement them yet.

Assign each existing page a job. The homepage introduces the promise. Work shows evidence. About gives context. A case study explains decisions and contribution. Your writing, visual choices, and action labels should reinforce the same message.

## 6. Test your interpretation

Give a classmate a short look at your homepage, then ask what you do, who your work serves, and where they would click next. Ask them to find one piece of evidence. Record their actual answers without coaching them. This is a small feedback exercise, not proof of broad conversion performance.

Revise one thing based on what you learn. Keep your completed brief in `docs/my-discovery-brief.md` so a future agent can understand the reasons behind your decisions.
